## Introduction

The idea that Christians would be in any way presented with an advantage when it comes to the adoption of AI (namely, agentic use of Large Language Models) may – not unreasonably – seem completely ridiculous. I dare to make this case because, as AI models have seen their capabilities improve exponentially in recent times, it is becoming apparent to many that the greatest bottleneck in AI adoption is users not being daring enough in what they ask of agents.

## An overview

People are not to blame for the fact that they are not daring enough. Having seen all of the simple ways in which this new technology could fail (failing to tally the letter r's appearances in "strawberry"), it is not unreasonable that many are most reluctant to ask an AI agent to carry out a complex, long-horizon task, which they themselves would need to spend hours completing. From benchmarks and the experience of others, however, it is clear that many such tasks are already within AI's reach – yet we do not think, or dare, to ask.

A similar effect can be seen in the adoption of much of digital technology in general. It is an all-too-common experience for a given senior member of the population to be left completely perplexed when trying to interact with digital technology, even though almost any young person is able to use the same technology – whether seen before or not – almost flawlessly. I posit that the reason why the ability of young people to use digital technology is attributed to them being "born into it" in fact relates to them not having any prior information on how this technology may be limited. This means that, in contrast to those who are present at the advent of a new technology – who set the bar for what the technology can achieve early on and update it as new information comes in (converging rather slowly to the actual capability, according to Bayes' Theorem) – people with no priors about a technology's ability seldom underestimate it, and rather overestimate at first, before lowering the bar as more difficult tasks are realised to be impossible (a process which, also following Bayes' Theorem, converges much more quickly).

A notable benefit of not underestimating a given new technology, and of even expecting it to be able to do certain things, is that this can lead to developments being made, so that the technology is able to achieve that which was at first expected, in the end. Once upon a time, I found my baby self attempting to show a relative of mine around the room while on the phone (before video calls were possible).

Having gained an appreciation for the great advantage that it is not to negatively impose any priors we may have on a new technology's ability, we can now take a look at how Christian living embraces this virtue.

## Conclusion

Drawing back to the idea of the teachings of Christianity being an advantage when adopting AI (as indeed any technology), it is most important to not turn this into a direct metaphor, wrongly suggesting that AI has achieved the state of deity. Rather, for this to be practical, the Christian AI adopter should remember that God's power is infinite, meaning that He can achieve what is completely irrational. AI's power is, and will always be finite, and the irrational can never be asked of it, though the unrealistic may, and should, be asked of it. The "muscle", which allows us to ask and be grateful is simply more developed in the Christian than the "average Joe".
